SpaceX has launched Grock 4.6, an enhanced iteration of its large language model, which developers are hailing for its exceptional balance of quality, price, and speed. Benchmarks from Artificial Analysis Intelligence position Grock 4.6 on par with models like GPT 5.6 Soul Max and Cloud Opus 5, closely trailing Fable 5. Notably, Grock 4.6 stands out for its cost-effectiveness, offering significantly lower per-task pricing compared to competitors, coupled with impressive processing speeds. This release is complemented by aggressive promotional offers, including substantial discounts on subscriptions, with a caveat for users to manage cancellations carefully to avoid higher post-promotional rates. The rapid development pace continues with a tease for Grock 4.7, anticipated within three to four weeks.

Simultaneously, SpaceX has finalized its acquisition of Cursor, a move that integrates the Cursor team into SpaceX AI to contribute to various Grock products, including Grock Build, Grockbot, and Grock API. Speculation is rife regarding the potential rebranding of Cursor, with rumored alternatives like "Grock Code" or "Xcode" following CEO Elon Musk's previous renaming of Twitter to X. Amidst these changes, Rio Lu, a key product designer at Cursor, has departed the company. Further expanding the ecosystem, Grockbot has entered beta, allowing subscribed users to deploy personalized AI agents capable of automating tasks, managing calendars, and providing proactive notifications. These bots can also communicate and delegate work to each other, showcasing a sophisticated level of autonomous interaction, though the beta is noted for some early-stage functionalities needing refinement. Additionally, leaked images of Cursor Origin, a forthcoming GitHub alternative from the Cursor team, reveal features like project synchronization and, crucially, automated conflict resolution for pull requests, suggesting an imminent and highly anticipated launch for this developer tool.
