Flock Safety, an $8.4 billion startup, operates an extensive network of AI-powered automated license plate readers (ALPRs) across thousands of communities, scanning billions of plates monthly. Its flagship "Falcon" camera leverages an Edge ML pipeline, where an on-device neural network classifies vehicle attributes like make, model, color, dents, rims, roof racks, and unique stickers, creating a "vehicle fingerprint." This structured metadata, rather than raw video, is then uploaded via LTE to the Flock Cloud, where it populates a nationwide searchable database. This system allows law enforcement to track vehicle movements, compare against "hot lists" for stolen cars or Amber Alerts, and receive real-time push notifications, effectively building a comprehensive "Marauder's Map" of American roadways. The aggregated data provides insights into vehicle owners' patterns, including residential, work, and visitation locations.

The widespread deployment of Flock's technology is largely enabled by the "third-party doctrine," a 1970s Supreme Court ruling stating that data voluntarily shared with a third party loses its reasonable expectation of privacy, allowing government access without a warrant. This legal loophole has been criticized for circumventing Fourth Amendment protections, especially given documented instances of misuse. Audits have revealed cases such as an Idaho sheriff accessing his wife's license plate over 700 times, Illinois officers running searches for federal immigration agents, and a Kansas police chief querying an ex-girlfriend's plates 164 times. Public backlash is mounting, with cities like Austin and Evanston opting to remove Flock cameras, and a Virginia lawsuit challenging its constitutionality. Despite these challenges, Flock Safety continues to expand its offerings into gunshot detection, police drones, and its "Flock OS," though a partnership with Amazon Ring was recently terminated due to privacy concerns. In response to the growing surveillance, community-led initiatives like "DFlock" are crowdsourcing the mapping of Flock camera locations, demonstrating a proactive effort to surveil the surveillance.
