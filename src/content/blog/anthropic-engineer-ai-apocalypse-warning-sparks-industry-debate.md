Former Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxson recently ignited a global debate after resigning on September 9th, citing profound fears that AI development is irresponsibly accelerating towards self-improving superintelligence, potentially leading to humanity's demise. Coxson, who accumulated three years of pre-training research experience at both OpenAI and Anthropic, cautioned against underestimating AI's rapidly accumulating power. His claims, initially met with skepticism across social media, were swiftly corroborated by former colleagues, most notably Evan Hubinger, Anthropic's Lead of Alignment Science. Hubinger not only confirmed Coxson's existence but publicly endorsed his core message, stating, "We sincerely believe that AI could kill all humans," and personally estimating a greater than 10% chance within the next decade. Coxson criticized the industry's "hubristic gamble," highlighting Anthropic's internal rationale of being "trapped in a race" to develop AI responsibly before others, despite the perceived existential risks.

This alarming discourse quickly escalated into broader discussions on AI governance and industry pace. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei publicly advocated for slowing frontier model advancements, proposing a three-part plan that included Anthropic's unilateral commitment to providing permanent, employee-level access for external evaluators to ensure safety and alignment. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman echoed this sentiment, agreeing to independent evaluator access and signaling coordination with Anthropic and Google on AI safety. However, this push for regulation has been met with complex reactions across political and economic spheres. An unusual bipartisan alliance, featuring Bernie Sanders and Steve Bannon, is advocating for AI restrictions, starkly contrasting with Donald Trump's view of such calls as a "sick conspiracy" hindering U.S. leadership. The rapid commoditization of AI, exemplified by 80% of new AI startups utilizing open models and Palantir achieving 95% cost reduction by fine-tuning open-source solutions, introduces a competitive dynamic. Industry analysts speculate that the push for stringent safety regulations, including OpenAI's delayed IPO citing "AI safety concerns" amid a reported 44% valuation drop on secondary markets, could also serve as a strategic effort to create barriers to entry, protect proprietary models, and consolidate market power within the industry's leading players.
