Nvidia co-founder and CEO Jensen Huang has resigned from his position, effective immediately, ending a 33-year tenure at the helm of the AI hardware giant. The resignation follows a tumultuous period ignited by Huang's admission of an extramarital affair, which U.S. intelligence alleges was with Alice Wong, a suspected operative for China's Ministry of State Security. Wong is also implicated in controlling Mega Speed, a Singapore-based company that controversially acquired over $4.8 billion in Nvidia's high-end GPUs over two years. This sophisticated scheme, developed after the late 2022 U.S. ban on chip sales to China, involved Mega Speed purchasing GPUs and racking them in data centers in Malaysia and Indonesia, then renting compute power to Alibaba — a method technically legal but under intense scrutiny for circumventing sanctions.

The scandal deepened following a 2024 Commerce Department inspection that found unopened Nvidia GPUs at a Mega Speed facility, suggesting potential redistribution. Short seller Culper Research's May 2026 report, "The China Problem," further exposed Mega Speed's explosive balance sheet growth and alleged financial ties to Alibaba through collateral agreements, despite Nvidia's claims of no Chinese shareholders. Political involvement surfaced with reports of Marco Rubio informing President Trump about Huang's relationship with a suspected spy. While Nvidia's board initially expressed confidence in Huang, new allegations emerged of another affair with Nvidia's VP of Healthcare, Kimberly Powell. This revelation prompted an emergency board meeting, leading to Huang's immediate departure. The company's CFO, Colette Kress, has been named interim CEO just days before its earnings call, as Nvidia's stock continues to plummet amidst shareholder lawsuits and a threatened congressional subpoena.
